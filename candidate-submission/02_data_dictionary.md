# Artifact 2: Data Dictionary

*Author: Teddy Desta*

This dictionary gives Brickline a shared business vocabulary without pretending that every common term has one enterprise record. Terms such as Customer, Order, Inventory, and Vehicle mean different things in different workflows. The dictionary makes those differences explicit, identifies the domain accountable for each definition, and records the aliases that consumers will encounter in the source systems.

The classification shown is the default for the canonical record represented by the term, not the sensitivity of the word itself. A public recall notice, for example, is **Public**, while a customer-to-VIN ownership record is **Restricted**. A field may inherit a stricter classification when it is combined with other data. That is especially important for VINs, customer identifiers, match results, and financial attributes.

| Term | Definition | Owning Domain | Classification | Synonyms / Aliases | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VIN** | The 17-character Vehicle Identification Number, per ISO 3779, is used to identify a specific vehicle. | Manufacturing | Internal | `vin` (Plant 1 and DMS), `vehicle_id` (Plant 2), `vehicle_vin_provided` (Commerce) | Publish VINs in uppercase after structural validation, but retain the submitted value and validation result for lineage. A VIN is not direct contact information, but it becomes sensitive when linked to a customer, owner, or purchase history. A valid non-Brickline VIN may appear in trade-in data without a Manufacturing match. |
| **Vehicle** | The physical car or truck as a business object identified by a VIN and described by facts such as manufacturer, model, build history, and operational status. | Manufacturing (for vehicles Brickline built) | Internal | — | Careful with this one. Manufacturing's "Vehicle" always means something Brickline built. Dealer Network's Trade-In Intake takes in vehicles Brickline never built — a customer's old BMW — and those get a VIN too. Don't assume every VIN you find in Dealer Network resolves back to a Manufacturing build record; a fair number never will, and that's expected, not broken data. |
| **Customer** | A person or business with a commercial relationship to Brickline, represented in the context in which that relationship was created. | Federated term: Dealer Network owns dealer buyers; Brickline Parts owns commerce accounts | Restricted | `customer_id` (both DMS and Commerce, different formats) | This is the trap everyone falls into. Dealer Network's Customer (`D-12345`) bought a vehicle. Commerce's Customer (`CU_a7b3c9`) has a login to the parts store — which includes dealers buying wholesale, i.e. businesses, not people. Same word, two tables, no shared key between them. James Patterson is literally both `D-12345` and `CU_a7b3c9` in the sample data today and Brickline has no way to know that without the Identity Resolution work in this design — which, yeah, is doing a lot of heavy lifting in that one clause. More on it in Artifact 1. |
| **Dealer Customer** | A person who financed or purchased a vehicle through a Brickline dealer. | Dealer Network | Restricted | `customer_id` (`D-XXXXX` in `sales_orders.csv`) | Gets created at the moment someone signs financing paperwork, not when they walk into a showroom. If you're counting "how many customers do we have," this number only includes people who actually bought — no browsing/lead data lives here. |
| **Commerce Customer** | An account holder on the Brickline Parts e-commerce platform — can be an individual vehicle owner or a business. | Brickline Parts | Restricted | `customer_id` (`CU_xxxxxx`) | The source table has a `customer_type` field (`B2B`/`B2C`) sitting right there, which tells you Brickline already half-knows these are two different things — a `B2B` row is a dealer's parts department, not a car owner, and it's being modeled identically to a retail buyer today. |
| **Order** | A completed purchase transaction. | *Split — Dealer Network for vehicle sales, Brickline Parts for parts orders* | Confidential | `sales_order_id` (Dealer Network), `order_id` (Commerce) | These aren't the same kind of thing wearing a different label. A Dealer Network order is one VIN, financing terms, five figures. A Commerce order is a cart with multiple SKUs, usually under $200, repeatable weekly. Resist the urge to build one unified "Order" table just because the English word matches — nothing about their shape, cardinality, or lifecycle does. I'll say it again if someone asks. |
| **Inventory** | Stock currently available to sell. | *Split — Dealer Network for vehicles, Brickline Parts for SKUs* | Internal | `inventory_snapshot.csv` (Dealer Network), `in_stock` (Commerce catalog) | Another word doing two jobs. Dealer Network's Inventory means "which built vehicles are physically sitting on which dealer's lot right now" (`days_on_lot`, one row per VIN). Commerce's Inventory means "do we have this part number in stock" (a boolean on the SKU). If someone in a meeting asks for "the inventory report" without saying which one, stop and ask — it's a different query against a different domain either way. |
| **Recall** | A manufacturer-issued notice that a defined set of vehicles has a safety or quality issue requiring remediation. | Manufacturing | Public | — | Doesn't exist as a system of record anywhere today — it happens by email and ad-hoc spreadsheet. Recalls are legally disclosable to owners and regulators, which is exactly why this is Public rather than Internal; there's no business reason to restrict it once it exists as governed data. This is the term the whole redesign has to invent, not just formalize — kind of wild there's zero system of record for something this legally consequential, but here we are. |
| **Vehicle Ownership** | The current (and historical) record of who holds title to a specific VIN. | Dealer Network | Confidential | — | Doesn't exist as tracked data today either. The closest thing is the most recent row in `sales_orders.csv`, which is never updated again after that sale — so the moment a vehicle changes hands a second time, or gets traded in, Brickline's records go stale silently. This gap is specifically what makes the CEO's recall question unanswerable right now. |
| **Dealer** | One of the ~200 independently owned/operated businesses that sell Brickline vehicles under a dealer agreement. | Dealer Network | Internal | `dealer_id` (`DLR-XXX`) | Don't confuse this with a Commerce B2B customer. A dealer's parts department buying wholesale from Brickline Parts shows up as a Commerce Customer with `customer_type = B2B` — a completely separate record with no link back to that dealer's `dealer_id`. Same real-world business, two disconnected identities. Worth fixing eventually; out of scope for the Level 3 design. Flagging it so it doesn't quietly get lost. |
| **Supplier / Vendor** | A company Manufacturing buys parts and components from. | Manufacturing | Internal | `supplier_id` (`SUP-XXX`, Plant 1), `vendor_code` (`S_xxxx`, Plant 2) | The same real-world supplier can carry two totally different IDs depending which plant is buying from them — there's no master supplier list today. Which means "how much do we spend with Supplier X" is currently a question you can only answer one plant at a time, by hand. |
| **Model / Model Code** | The vehicle model/trim line, e.g. `CIVIC-LX`. | Manufacturing | Internal | `model_code` (Plant 1), `model` (Plant 2), `compatible_models` (Commerce) | This is the quieter cross-domain seam nobody talks about besides VIN — Commerce's entire parts-compatibility engine depends on its `compatible_models` values matching Manufacturing's model codes exactly, letter for letter, and there's no shared reference list enforcing that today. A typo in either system just silently breaks fitment lookups. |
| **Production Order** | A single vehicle's build record as it moves through a plant. | Manufacturing | Internal | `production_order_id` (Plant 1), `mo_id` (Plant 2) | The column-name mismatch is the easy part. The dates are worse: Plant 1 uses `YYYY-MM-DD`, Plant 2 uses `YYYY/MM/DD`, and Plant 1's own QC file has three different date formats mixed in the same column depending on who typed it into Excel that week. Never trust a date's format without checking which source system it came from first. |
| **QC Severity** | How serious a quality issue found on a vehicle is. | Manufacturing | Internal | `severity` = `minor`/`major`/`critical` (Plant 1), `level` = `LOW`/`MED`/`HIGH` (Plant 2) | These roughly map to each other but that mapping isn't written down anywhere today — someone just "knows" major is about a MED, give or take. Not good enough once you actually want to compare defect rates across plants, which is the whole point of having two plants report into one company. |
| **Trade-In** | A vehicle a customer hands over as partial payment toward a new purchase, usually not a Brickline vehicle. | Dealer Network | Confidential | `received_vin`, `trade_in_vin` | The VIN on a trade-in is exactly as real as any other VIN — it's just one Manufacturing will never have a build record for, since Brickline didn't build it. See the Vehicle entry above. Appraised value is the sensitive part here, which is why this is Confidential rather than plain Internal. |
| **Part Number / SKU** | An identifiable component (going into a vehicle) or a sellable accessory/replacement item (sold after the fact). | Brickline Parts (SKU, the catalog unit) — also appears in Manufacturing's receiving data | Internal | `part_number` (Manufacturing receiving), `sku` (Plant 2 receiving, Commerce catalog) | Don't assume these are the same numbering scheme just because the values look similar. A `part_number` on Manufacturing's receiving dock is a component going *into* a vehicle on the line; a `sku` in the Parts catalog is a boxed item sold *after* the vehicle already exists. They may well overlap for some items, but there's no confirmed crosswalk between the two today — that's a real open question for whoever owns Product Catalog, and not one I could settle just from the sample data. |
| **Shipment** | The physical delivery of a parts order to a customer. | Brickline Parts | Internal | `shipment_id` | Purely a Commerce concept — has nothing to do with how a finished vehicle gets from a plant to a dealer's lot (that's the Vehicle Build → Lot Inventory handoff, a completely different workflow). Don't let "shipping" get overloaded across the two. |
| **Finance Type** | How a vehicle purchase was paid for. | Dealer Network | Confidential | `finance_type` = `cash`/`loan`/`lease` | Tied directly to an individual customer's purchase, which is why it's Confidential rather than Internal even though it's just three enum values. |
| **Plant** | One of Brickline's two manufacturing facilities. | Manufacturing | Internal | `plant_code`, `plant` (`P1`/`P2`) | Two entirely separate ERPs sit behind this one word. Nothing about "Plant" is shared infrastructure today — not the schema, not the ID formats, not even the date formatting convention. |

## Federated Ownership Rules

I have added 19 terms to the Data Dictionary. I could have stretched it to include Warranty and Service History as both come up in the scenario — but neither has anything backing it in the sample data pack, and I'd rather leave that gap flagged than invent a canonical definition for data that doesn't exist yet.
The dictionary is centrally discoverable but not centrally authored in isolation. Each owning domain approves its definitions, aliases, classifications, and quality rules. Cross-domain terms such as Customer, Order, Inventory, and Part/SKU require agreement from all contributing domains, with a named steward responsible for resolving disputes and recording decisions.

Changes should be versioned and reviewed because a definition change can affect schemas, quality rules, access policies, and reports. The catalog should show the approved definition alongside product-level implementation details so consumers can see both the business meaning and how a specific product represents it.

## Classification Rules Used

- **Public** — approved for release outside Brickline, such as a published recall campaign.
- **Internal** — operational or reference data intended for Brickline personnel and approved partners.
- **Confidential** — commercially sensitive data or persistent identifiers whose misuse or combination could expose business or personal activity.
- **Restricted** — direct personal data or records that link an identifiable person to ownership, contact, financial, or transaction information.

These are default classifications. Product and field-level policies may be stricter based on context, jurisdiction, purpose of use, or linkage to other records. The strictest applicable control governs access at runtime.
